# 06 — Governance & Ownership

The layer that decides whether everything else survives the founder. The goal is
**equilibrium**: distributed power so the system can resist its own founder, its own
monopolies, and its own success — and "push back from the bottom."

## Differentiated ownership — one form does not fit all

The biggest design error would be a single ownership form. Match the form to the hardness
test (`00`, Principle 3):

| Layer | Form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Land** | **Community Land Trust (CLT)** | Permanently decommodifies the ground — stops speculation from later capturing the value you create. Highest-leverage single move. |
| **Housing** | CLT-leased ownership + co-op rental | Residents build equity; land stays in trust (no bubble). |
| **Competitive businesses** (shops, services, factories, farms) | **Worker co-ops, federated** | The **Mondragon** model (80k worker-owners, own bank + university); federation means one co-op's failure doesn't sink the system. |
| **Essential monopolies** (water, power grid, the grocery) | Community trust / public-utility governance | Do **not** casually co-op-ize a monopoly — a captured monopoly co-op is just a new boss. Needs constitutional governance. |

Anchor pattern: the **Cleveland model** (Evergreen Cooperatives) — anchor institutions buy
from local worker co-ops to recirculate wealth. Use **multi-stakeholder boards** (workers
+ residents + consumers) so no single class dominates.

## The Blackbird co-op as the food-system template

Blackbird Coop's three-entity structure maps directly onto the geometry and ownership
stack:

| Blackbird entity | Honeycomb equivalent |
|---|---|
| Blackbird Farms (shared processing/marketing) | Community-owned processing co-op at **shared hex vertices** |
| Member farms | **Soft, local producers** (farm hexes + households) |
| Blackbird Logistics | Freight network on the **hex edges** |
| Blackbird Investments (holding) | The **CLT/trust** holding shared assets |

Shared processing/logistics = hard collective; individual production = soft local. Same
line again.

## The constitution (the hard governance shell)

Short, hard-to-amend, fixing **only** the load-bearing rules:
- The **hardness test** itself, and the priority ordering and principle stack (`00`).
- The **ownership split** (CLT owns land + monopolies; union owns operations + infill).
- **Caps** (pay ratios, town size).
- **Exit/voice rights** and the (slow) amendment process.
- The **transfer triggers** that bind the founder (below).

Keep it minimal — a fat constitution is a high-modernist plan in disguise. Hand each town
the *objective function and criteria* (hard, shared), not the *answers* (soft, local).

## The founder's paradox — bind your own hands first

You use *concentrated* power ($30B + total ownership) to build a system designed to
*disperse* power. The handoff is the most failure-prone moment (Pullman is the canonical
corpse: power stayed fused in the founder's hands).

**Resolution, encoded as a framework primitive:**
- **The first thing you make hard is your own exit.** Pre-commit the transfer legally —
  vesting schedules, **automatic conversion triggers**, CLT and co-op charters written
  *before* move-in.
- "Always on offer to sell to the union" is only credible if it is **structurally binding,
  not discretionary.** If the transfer happens when *you* decide, you *are* Pullman until
  you choose otherwise. Bind your hands while you still have the power to.
- Same logic extends to funders (`02`): the system must become free of whoever pays for it,
  just as it becomes free of whoever founds it. Non-coercion is required by the objective
  function itself (`01`).

## Equilibrium & the homeostat

Equilibrium is active, not static. The hard shell includes the feedback mechanisms:
- **Monitoring** (is power concentrating? is the water node secure?).
- The union's **countervailing power**; **exit rights**; **caps**; the **amendment process**.
- **Cap-and-spawn, not clone-and-paste** — small enough for the union to govern; let towns
  diverge in culture/specialization (Mondragon's co-ops share structure, do different
  things). A town that's grown its own character draws people and resists replacement.

Judge every mechanism by one test: *does this let the system resist its own founder, its
own monopolies, and its own success?*

## The one point to guard hardest

Geometry distributes power across the lattice but **cannot distribute the coastal water
node** (`03`). It is the single capture point an adversary or captured faction would seize.
Concentrate *protection* there: deepest trust, widest governing coalition, most binding
pre-commitments. Everything else the honeycomb protects for you; the water source it
cannot.

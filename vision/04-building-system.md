# 04 — Building System

Two materials and one design code. The structural load path *is* the hard/soft line.

## Shells: Quonset huts (galvalume steel arches)

Durable, cheap, low-maintenance, desert-suited.

- **Lifespan:** galvalume-coated steel ~**40–70 years**; reflective cool-roof finish
  reflects 50–70% of solar (cuts the desert cooling load).
- **No flat roof:** the arch sheds rain by geometry — eliminates the #1 commercial
  maintenance failure (flat-membrane leaks). Clear-span interior = reconfigurable.
- **Cost:** shell ~$9–15/sq ft; grocery/commercial all-in ~$120–180/sq ft (the
  refrigeration/electrical guts cost the same in any building — the shell is where you save
  ~40% vs. conventional structure).

**Desert-tuned, maintenance-optimized spec** (the single biggest lever is a *standardized
fleet* — one or two store/building models statewide → interchangeable parts, one trained
crew, bulk spares, centralized IoT monitoring):
- Galvalume + Kynar reflective finish; minimize roof penetrations; **no roof-mounted
  equipment** — HVAC/refrigeration on **ground-level concrete skids** beside the building
  (service without a crane, no roof leaks).
- Closed-cell spray-foam or aircrete lining (insulation + vapor barrier + rigidity).
- Sealed/polished concrete slab floor (near-zero maintenance). LED + sealed tubular
  daylighting. Rooftop/canopy **solar** to offset the cooling load.

## Infill: tunable aircrete

A **dial, not a single material.** One base chemistry (cement + foaming surfactant +
xanthan-gum stabilizer + water — recipe validated; xanthan stabilizes the soap bubbles so
they survive contact with wet cement) tuned along three axes:
**permeability ↔ insulation ↔ strength**, with additives buying strength back after you
open the pores.

| Property | Aircrete (low-density) | Normal structural concrete |
|---|---|---|
| Density | ~22 lb/ft³ | ~150 lb/ft³ |
| Compressive strength | ~1,000–2,000 psi (far lower at low density) | 3,000–5,000 psi |
| **Insulation** | **~R-6 / inch** | ~R-0.1 / inch |
| Workability | cut/carved with hand tools | power tools |
| Fire | non-combustible | non-combustible |

**Tunings → jobs** (all on the *non-structural / infill* side of the load path):

| Tune | Product | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Closed-cell, low-density | Insulating board (R-6/in) | Thermal lining of the Quonset shells |
| **Open-cell, water-wicking** | **Evaporative cooling wall / downdraught tower** | **Passive desert AC** — drops air temp 10–20°F, no compressor; offloads the solar/energy budget; adds comfort humidity to dry air |
| Permeable + additive-boosted | High-strength pervious (17–22 MPa achievable) | Monsoon drainage paving + groundwater recharge |
| Vapor-permeable | "Breathable" wall | Moisture management, no trapped condensation/mold |
| Open-cell, high surface area | Filter medium | Greywater / water-treatment substrate |

**Additive palette** (silica fume preserves permeability while boosting strength):

| Additive | Dose | Buys | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Silica fume | ≤20% | Strength, permeability preserved | Cheap byproduct — top pick |
| Fly ash | up to ~75% | Strength, durability, lower carbon | Cheap byproduct (serves green + cheap) |
| Nano-silica | ~1.5–2% | Largest strength jump | Pricey, finicky — sparing use |
| Fibers (PP/glass/PVA) | ≤1.5% | Tensile/flexural, crack control | Cheap, high value |

**QC guardrails (non-negotiable, per priority ordering Durable-first):** batch
compressive-strength testing; **fiber reinforcement** (shrinkage-crack control); **render
all weather-exposed faces** (aircrete absorbs water); a proper foam generator for
consistent bubble size at scale; keep the load path in the dedicated frame (don't load-bear
on low-density aircrete). Soap-based non-autoclaved aircrete is *weaker* than autoclaved
AAC — don't borrow AAC's reputation.

**Vertical-integration loops:** fly ash/silica fume are industrial byproducts; brine-
recovered Mg is the basis of MgO (Sorel) cements (a coherent brine→Mg→binder loop, *if*
MgO's water-durability is solved).

## The design code: fix / free / govern

Research-grounded (full evidence in `09`). The decision is **not "fixed vs. free" — it is
"fix the *collective-impact* elements firmly, free the *private-impact* elements
generously."** The failure mode is never too much private freedom; it is *ungoverned*
freedom spilling into the commons (light, shared space, the public face) — the Quinta
Monroy lesson.

| Building attribute | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Structure, utilities, circulation, shared frame | **FIX** | Open Building support layer; enables decades of adaptation (NEXT21) |
| Envelope rules protecting neighbors' light/air & the commons | **FIX** | Quinta Monroy: ungoverned infill → windowless rooms, encroachment, conflict |
| Active ground-floor frontage + articulation/transparency minimums | **FIX** | Facade research: variety/transparency lifts mood, dwell, walking, safety |
| Coherent framing rhythm (alignment, street wall, datums) | **FIX (lightly)** | Variety is an inverted-U — chaos is as bad as monotony |
| Interior layout, fit-out, use, expansion within envelope | **FREE** | Flexibility → wellbeing (autonomy/competence/relatedness) |
| Facade color/material/detail/style within the frame | **FREE** | Personalization → place attachment + self-esteem |
| What business/use fills the space | **FREE** | Jacobs' diversity; sites-and-services 20-yr success |

**Narrow externality covenants** are the *only* place uniformity is justified (HOA ~4%
price premium = buyers paying for credible commitment against neighbor defection). Enforce
**pre-set rules consistently** — HOA conflict comes from *selective* enforcement and
process, not goals. Don't over-engineer flexibility either (Schneider & Till: elaborate
"hard" flexibility goes unused; cheap "soft" loose-fit, slightly-oversized generic rooms
serve better).

### Why aircrete makes the design code *safe*

Aircrete's structural weakness is a **feature**: because it is non-structural, residents can
cut, carve, and personalize their own aircrete infill **without endangering the building.**
The steel/RC frame carries the load (fixed, professional); the aircrete is everything else
(free, resident-shaped). **The load path is the hard/soft line, made physical.**

*Guardrail (don't over-attribute):* Pruitt-Igoe failed from socioeconomics/funding, not
modernist design — don't blame geometry/uniformity for what are money and governance
failures.

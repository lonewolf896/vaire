# 02 — Capital & Funding: The Treasury Constitution

The survival mechanism. The funding strategy and the endowment glidepath are not finance
details — they are how the system becomes independent and un-killable.

## The inflow: leech the leeches (a market solution to a market problem)

A high-value AI-efficiency invention (claimed ~10× faster inference, ~98% less VRAM),
licensed to AI companies at **~20% of their prior-year AI spend, US-only** (export
compliance). The collection enforcer is **the AI industry's own competitive greed**: if
the tech is real, *not* licensing it is competitive death, so each player pays to deny
rivals the edge. You tax their fear of each other.

**Reframe: a tollbooth, not a leech.** A parasite kills the host or gets removed; a toll on
a road they want to drive is durable because they keep paying for genuine value, with a
redistribution premium baked in. Precedent: university tech-transfer (e.g., WARF's
vitamin-D patents funding decades of research).

### Revenue sizing (illustrative, grounded — see `09`)

US AI infrastructure/compute spend, 2025: hyperscaler AI capex ~$240–270B US-only (of
~$388B global Big-Four capex), plus the labs. 20% of the defensible base ≈ **$60–80B**
gross; the plan deliberately uses a **very conservative working budget**:

- **Year 1: ~$30B** (haircut for friction, contestation, ramp).
- **Ongoing: ~$15B/yr** (price drops after year 1; uncertain and likely declining).

The exact extraction is **unknown** — which is *why* the budget is conservative and why the
glidepath below makes the uncertainty survivable.

## The threat: their greed cuts both ways

The same greed that makes them pay also hunts for the exit:
- **Design around / reverse-engineer / challenge the patent / infringe-and-litigate** — big
  labs can outspend you in court.
- **Retaliate** — acquire you, lobby/regulate, starve the project.
- **Concentration risk** — a flourishing engine funded by a handful of giants is the exact
  fragile dependency the equilibrium design forbids everywhere else.

**Defenses:**
1. **Make the toll un-removable** — deliver the tech as a **service** (API/SaaS), secret
   sauce server-side: nothing to reverse-engineer, only access to rent. Network effects +
   continuous improvement widen the moat.
2. **The deeper one:** treat the income as the **bootstrap, not the foundation** (below).

## The endowment glidepath (the core rule)

> **Never build an obligation you haven't endowed.**

Every increment of buildout (each hex/town) is bundled with the permanent capital to fund
it forever, locked *before or as* it comes online. This converts catastrophic uncertainty
into benign: if income stops, you simply **stop expanding**, and everything already built
is **funded in perpetuity**. Income uncertainty changes only *how big* you get — never
*whether what you built survives.*

### Perpetuity math

To fund an annual subsidy **S** forever:

> **Principal = S ÷ (real draw rate)**

- At a conservative **~3.5% real draw → ~28–30× S.** (3% → ~33×; 4% → 25×.)
- **Draw only *real* returns; reinvest the inflation portion** so principal grows with the
  wage/cost inflation it funds. Nominal draws bleed out over decades.
- Given the "never fail" ethos, lean **3–3.5% real**.

### Growth taxes itself

Every town added permanently raises the endowment bar by ~28–30× its subsidy. A given income
level therefore **naturally caps** scale. This is a feature: the finance independently
enforces the governance design (cap-and-spawn, stay-bounded, equilibrium). **Replicate,
don't bloat.**

## The financial constitution (robust to any income)

Lexicographic priority for every year's income (same shape as the priority ordering —
Durable-first = solvent-first):

| Priority | Claim on income | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Operating subsidy for what exists | Keep living people whole — non-negotiable |
| 2 | Liquidity reserve (1–2 yrs of subsidy) | A sudden income drop can't catch you exposed |
| 3 | Close the endowment gap on everything built | Make the existing system income-stop-proof |
| 4 | **Only then:** build + endow one new increment | Growth is the residual, never the priority |

At **every moment**, if the leech vanishes, everything built is permanently funded.
**Solvency-first, growth-second.**

## Front-load the locking — exploit the window

The window between *"they must pay"* and *"they've found the exit"* will close. So:
- **Fat years (esp. year-1's $30B):** over-endow, lock aggressively. Early income is worth
  more than later income (later may not come).
- **Lean years:** slow the building; **never** trade endowment coverage for build speed.
- This is "Fast is last" in financial form — sacrifice expansion velocity to buy permanent
  independence during the only window you're guaranteed.

## Sequence as a cheap test

The income is the biggest unknown, so:
1. **Sign the first licenses → observe the actual dollars.** That prices everything
   downstream (the cheap test for the income variable).
2. Size Phase 1 to what's real, not hoped.
3. **"Town One, permanently funded"** before Town Two. Build-and-endow one town (or small
   cluster) at a time, each fully secured before the next.

## Scenario robustness

| Scenario | Outcome under the rule |
|---|---|
| **Pessimistic** (little extracted, short window) | A small seed — one or two towns — but rock-solid, funded forever. A win, not a collapse. |
| **Central** ($30B + $15B/yr for some years) | Steady build-and-endow to a meaningful federation. |
| **Optimistic** (large extraction, long window) | Many towns, large endowment, broad cap-and-spawn. |

Solvency is guaranteed in every branch; only scale moves.

## Default knobs (proposed — confirm in `10`)

- **Real draw rate: 3.5%** (≈28× multiple).
- **Liquidity reserve: 2 years** of operating subsidy.
- **Batch size: one town at a time** (maximally safe).

*Illustrative: a ~12k-person town's subsidy may land ~$10–50M/yr (pilots will pin it) →
~$0.3–1.4B perpetuity capital each. Year-1's $30B could permanently endow several towns
outright. Placeholders until the first licenses and the first town's real operating cost
are measured.*

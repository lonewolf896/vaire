# 00 — The Principle Stack

The constitution of thought. Five principles, each answering a different question. They
are themselves part of the **hard shell** (see Principle 3): shared, slow to change, and
inherited by every town and successor. Specific choices stay soft and local — valid as
long as they satisfy these.

---

## Principle 1 — Objective Function (the *Why*)

**Optimize for human flourishing, because secure, happy, free minds produce faster and
better science.** The settlement is instrumental; cognition is the product.

Grounded in three converging literatures (see `01` and `09`):
- **Scarcity is a cognitive tax** — financial fear drops effective cognition ~13–14 IQ
  points (Mullainathan & Shafir).
- **Positive affect broadens cognition** — happiness widens attention, flexibility,
  creativity (Fredrickson's broaden-and-build; Isen).
- **Safety + autonomy drive innovation** — psychological safety is the #1 predictor of
  high-performing teams (Edmondson; Google Project Aristotle).

**Hard constraint that falls out of it:** the science must be an *emergent gift, never a
demanded quota.* Demanding output re-introduces the fear that destroys output (Goodhart).
Therefore **non-coercion is structurally required by the goal itself** — the ethics and
the efficacy are the same constraint.

---

## Principle 2 — Priority Ordering (the *What matters*)

When choices conflict, resolve in this order:

> **Durable > Green-end-state > Cheap > Fast**

Precise reading (the parenthetical matters):
- **Durable** — hard requirement on *every* choice. No exceptions. Durable-first means
  *solvent-first* in finance and *lasts-70-years* in materials.
- **Green** — hard requirement on the **destination**, soft preference on the **path**.
  The trajectory *must* converge green; the transition may be dirty (e.g., grid power
  now, solar later; Portland cement now, geopolymer/low-carbon binder later).
- **Cheap** — optimize *within* the durable + green-converging set. Budget on **lifecycle
  cost, not first cost**.
- **Fast** — tiebreaker only. Sacrificed first. *You scale by replication, not haste.*

---

## Principle 3 — Externally Hard, Locally Soft (the *Where*)

> **Soft by default, hard by necessity.**

An element joins the **hard shell** only if it passes one of three tests:
1. **Externality** — its failure imposes costs on others beyond the actor.
2. **Natural monopoly** — water, power, rail, land.
3. **Load-bearing** — survival of the system depends on it (the capital base, the
   constitution itself).

Everything else is **soft**: local, individual, free to vary, free to fail. The discipline
is *minimizing the hard set* — every piece of rigidity must justify itself against the
three tests, or it becomes soft.

**Hard ≠ frozen. Hard = slow.** Use pace-layering (Brand's shearing layers; Habraken's
levels): hard layers change slowly and by broad consent; soft layers change fast and
freely. The hard shell must contain the (slow) rules for how the soft layer may amend it
— otherwise "hard" becomes a brittle cage and the system cannot adapt or push back from
the bottom.

The recurring boundary, at every scale, is the **line of collective impact** — and in
buildings it is literally the **structural load path** (see `04`).

---

## Principle 4 — Epistemics (the *How to know*)

> **Buy information before commitment. Rigor is a speed multiplier.**

- Always run the **cheapest/fastest/easiest test that collapses the biggest uncertainty**,
  before spending capital. Information is cheap; commitment is expensive. Never invert.
- Every variable you have *tested* is one you no longer hedge, re-litigate, or carry as
  risk. Trusted variables compound into speed. Rigor early → speed late.
- Corollary: **design the experiments, not the plant.** The first move on any capital-heavy
  decision is the pilot that turns its gating guesses into trusted numbers (see `08`).

---

## Principle 5 — Bootstrapping (the *How to move*)

> **Secure footing, then ascend. One platform at a time.**

The master pattern, fractal across every layer (capital, material, energy, water, food,
governance, space, epistemics, cognition — see `07`):

- **Every platform needs an explicit load test** before you step up. "Secure" must be a
  criterion (endowed / strength-tested / measured / triggers-fired), never a feeling.
- **Every rung must be terminal-viable** — a complete stopping point. If everything halts
  here, what you have must still stand on its own. No cantilevers that only make sense if
  the next rung gets built.
- **Anti-stall:** "secure" means *bears load*, not *flawless*. Set the threshold, hit it,
  move. The closing funding window forces pace — don't camp on a platform.
- **Dependency order:** map the chain; start at the break-in point (the prerequisite-free
  first move); kill circular dependencies.
- The method embodies the goal: bootstrapping is the **low-fear, anti-fragile, never-bet-
  the-farm** way to build — it models the security it aims to produce.

---

## How they relate

```
        WHY            flourishing → faster science          (Principle 1)
         │
   constrained by      Durable > Green > Cheap > Fast         (Principle 2)
         │
   structured by       externally hard, locally soft          (Principle 3)
         │
   de-risked by        buy information before commitment       (Principle 4)
         │
   executed by         bootstrap: secure footing, ascend       (Principle 5)
```

Purpose, priorities, structure, knowledge, tempo — a closed set. Everything else in this
plan is an application of these five.
